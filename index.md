Genome sequencing is now a routine part of outbreak investigations. This training covers how to turn microbial genomes into evidence for public health action: identifying and typing organisms, measuring how closely isolates are related, building and reading phylogenetic trees, and interpreting the results alongside epidemiological data.

The material is organized into six connected sections. Each one pairs short **lectures** with **hands-on activities** that use real public data and the command-line tools used in public health laboratories.

Developed by the {{ site.organization }} for laboratories in the Western Region, and open to anyone working in public health genomics.
