This site hosts **{{ site.title }}**, a training course developed by the **{{ site.organization }}**. It was written for public health laboratories and epidemiology programs in the Western Region, but the material is not region-specific and is open to anyone working in public health genomics.

## Who this course is for

- **Laboratory scientists and bioinformaticians** who generate or analyze genomic data for surveillance and outbreak response
- **Epidemiologists** who need to understand and interpret genomic results in an investigation
- Anyone who wants a practical foundation in microbial population genomics and phylogenetics

Comfort with a command-line terminal is helpful for the hands-on activities. The lectures do not require it.

## What the course covers

1. **Foundations of Microbial Population Genomics:** evolution, mutation, recombination, population structure, and species delineation
2. **Taxonomic Classification and Genomic Subtyping:** species identification, MLST, k-mer methods, and other typing approaches
3. **High-Resolution Genomic Relatedness:** cgMLST, core-SNP analysis, genomic distance, and clustering
4. **Phylogenetic Methods and Tree Building:** evolutionary models, tree-building methods, and tree interpretation
5. **Interpreting Phylogenomic Evidence in Outbreak Investigations:** clusters, background diversity, and the limits of transmission inference
6. **Integrated Genomic Epidemiology Case Studies:** bringing typing, relatedness, phylogenetics, and epidemiology together

Sections build on each other, so working through them in order is recommended. Sections that are still in development are marked as forthcoming.

## How to use this site

- **Follow the sections in order.** The *Next* link at the bottom of every page takes you to the next lecture or activity, and on into the next section.
- **Activities are step by step.** Tick off each step as you finish it; the progress bar at the top of the activity tracks where you are.
- **Answer the questions as you go.** Check your answer, ask for a hint, or reveal the answer if you are stuck.
- **Copy commands with one click.** Every code block has a *Copy* button. Blocks labeled *Terminal* are meant to be run in your shell.

Your progress is stored only in your own browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
